# Storm-restoration social campaigns

Use this skill to take a local roofing or restoration campaign from brief review through creative delivery and lead-path readiness. Use it for paid-social packages and related organic captions. Do not publish or activate an ad campaign unless the user separately authorizes that action.

## Workflow

1. **Review the brief.** Identify audience, service area, campaign angle, offer, destination, CTA, deliverables, sizes, duration, and prohibited claims. Retain exact copy the user requires. Clarify only ambiguities that change the deliverable.
2. **Check facts and brand assets.** Use the official website and user-provided materials to confirm service area, contact route, offer, and positioning. Use the official logo file as a supplied image asset; never substitute an AI redraw or invented company mark. Do not invent prices, review counts, credentials, project outcomes, testimonials, guarantees, or insurer relationships.
3. **Gate the lead path before launch.** Review the destination and form status. If the page is a mockup, does not submit, lacks a CRM route, or has incomplete consent/privacy review, mark paid traffic **NO-GO**. Creative generation may continue, but keep the blocker prominent and recommend a verified alternative destination. Use `references/launch-readiness.md` for minimum checks.
4. **Plan the creative set.** Match the requested deliverables and ratios. Common defaults are one 15-second vertical video and three standalone static concepts in 4:5 and 1:1. Follow current media tools’ model, duration, ratio, resolution, reference, and plan restrictions. Never promise an unsupported version. When reframing, preserve key content and explain padding/cropping choices.
5. **Generate and brand.** Use the required media-generation workflow. Keep weather imagery realistic and non-catastrophic. Place the supplied official logo asset for exact brand identity. Keep overlay copy short and mobile-legible; preserve exact names, offers, location terms, and CTA. Do not imply an ad or photograph can diagnose roof damage.
6. **Draft social copy.** Provide multiple primary-text/caption alternatives and concise platform headlines/descriptions for each asset group. Keep claims factual and measured. Avoid personal-attribute assumptions, fear, guarantees, insurer-affiliation claims, and unverified offers. Distinguish paid copy from optional organic hashtags.
7. **Verify the output.** Check exact copy, official logo, dimensions/ratio, duration, audio, and playable file integrity. Review representative opening, middle, and CTA/end-card frames. Confirm every static ad is a separate file. Fix only clear acceptance failures.
8. **Deliver with a launch note.** Link every final asset once. State material media limitations and unresolved funnel blockers. Do not claim a form, CRM, consent flow, or conversion event is production-ready without an end-to-end test.

## Storm-claim guardrails

- Say weather **may** affect a roof and some conditions may not be visible from the ground; do not say damage is certain.
- Describe an inspection as an assessment of observed conditions, not a diagnosis from an ad, photo, or automated tool.
- Never guarantee insurance approval, coverage, payout, claim success, repair outcome, or specific cost.
- Do not imply the contractor is an insurer or acts on an insurer’s behalf.
- Keep urgency relevant and calm. Avoid catastrophic destruction unless the brief specifically requires it.
- Treat consent language, call/text permissions, privacy disclosures, and retention as matters for the business and qualified counsel to approve.
